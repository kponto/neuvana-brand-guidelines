# Objection handling

None of these has approved answer copy. The entries record the sources' stated direction only.

**"Does this really work?"**
- Source: “Reassure that stem is happening. 'Does this really work?'” (Dave). “John Q. Public sees it as mind control/snake oil” (Kermit). Benefits are “gradual and easily confounded”.
- Direction: show, don't assert. Use customer verbatims, correlational app insights, and one plain teaching layer. [INCOMPLETE]

**"Will it knock me out?"**
- Source: “Other solutions can knock you out - but how did you wake up?” and “We take melatonin, until we get used to that.” (Cassie).
- Direction: the workshop line “help restore balance without impairment”. [INCOMPLETE]

**"It's complicated" / "I'm not techy"**
- Source: “Technically unsavvy users are a problem - can't figure it out.” (Cassie). The app menu is “akin to 'restaurant' - nothing is labeled” (Ami). “Too many settings” (Tracy).
- Direction: this is a product issue. Pairing instructions move to support docs. [INCOMPLETE]

**"Another gadget that overpromises"**
- Source: Pulsetto “makes all the outrageous claims” (Cassie). “BS claims/noise” (Kermit).
- Direction: no overclaiming, and discounts stay rare.

**Subscription and price**
- Source: existing hardware owners keep lifetime premium access; new customers get a trial, then subscribe; “Nothing currently available to existing users is gated or removed.” The membership is framed “as ongoing value, not a toll”.
